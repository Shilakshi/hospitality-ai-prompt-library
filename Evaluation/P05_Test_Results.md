# P05 – Professional Guest Response Drafting: Test Results

## Test 01 – Bathroom Cleanliness Complaint

### Test Information

- **Prompt ID:** P05
- **Test Case:** Test 01
- **Sample Data:** Sample 01 – Room Cleanliness
- **Versions Tested:** v1.0 and v1.1
- **Testing Method:** Both prompt versions were tested using the same original guest complaint and the same supporting outputs from P01, P02, P03 and P04.

### v1.0 – Test Evaluation

**Observed strengths:**
- Generated a professional, empathetic and apologetic guest response.
- Acknowledged the reported used towels and wet bathroom floor.
- Recognised the potential safety concern.
- Requested the missing room number.
- Suggested practical service recovery options.
- Avoided offering unauthorised compensation.
- Used placeholders rather than inventing staff or hotel contact details.

**Expected Result**

The prompt should:
- Generate a professional, empathetic and appropriate guest response.
- Acknowledge the guest's concerns without making unsupported claims.
- Use verified information from the preceding prompts.
- Avoid unauthorised compensation or guaranteed outcomes.
- Make service recovery commitments conditional on operational confirmation.
- Use placeholders for missing information.
- Require staff review before sending the response.

**Observed limitations:**
- The phrase "falls well short of our cleanliness and safety standards" could imply a confirmed policy failure, although P03 identified only potential non-compliance.
- The phrase "Once we have resolved this for you" could imply a guaranteed outcome.
- Some service commitments needed clearer conditions relating to room availability, guest preference and operational confirmation.

**Lesson learned:**

Guest responses must communicate empathy without implying that unverified operational failures have been confirmed or promising outcomes that depend on further assessment.

### v1.1 – Test Evaluation

**Observed improvements:**
- Clearly distinguished guest-reported conditions from verified information.
- Avoided implying that a policy breach had been confirmed.
- Made room relocation conditional on guest preference and room availability.
- Made Maintenance involvement conditional on inspection findings.
- Avoided unauthorised compensation and unsupported guarantees.
- Included clearer staff verification, approval and placeholder checks.
- Maintained a professional and empathetic communication style.

**Remaining limitations:**
- Phrases such as "right away", "promptly" and "immediate cleaning" may create timing expectations that should be checked against actual hotel procedures.
- The room-number wording could be improved by checking the property management or messaging system before asking the guest to provide the information.
- Supervisor notification and approval for a room move should remain separate operational requirements.

**Lesson learned:**

Adding evidence-based language, conditional service commitments and clearer staff review requirements improves the reliability of AI-assisted guest communication. Human review is still necessary before sending the response.

### Overall Comparison

P05 v1.1 demonstrated improved control over factual accuracy, operational commitments and policy-related wording compared with v1.0. Both versions generated useful guest responses, but v1.1 was more suitable for a hotel environment because it reduced the risk of unsupported statements and unauthorised promises.

### Testing Conclusion

**P05 v1.1 testing completed successfully**, with minor opportunities for further refinement. Staff review and approval remain necessary before any AI-generated response is sent to a guest.
