# P07 – Hotel Accommodation and F&B Complaint Trend Analysis

## 1. Prompt Information

- **Prompt ID:** P07
- **Prompt Name:** Hotel Accommodation and F&B Complaint Trend Analysis
- **Version:** v1.1
- **Status:** Revised draft – Tested successfully.
- **Industry:** Hospitality
- **Business Areas:** Hotel Accommodation and Food & Beverage (F&B)

## 2. Intended Workflow or Task

To analyse multiple guest complaints and feedback records to identify recurring issues, complaint patterns and operational trends across hotel accommodation and Food & Beverage (F&B) departments.

This prompt can use individual guest complaint classifications and extracted information from P01 and P02, where available.

It groups feedback into relevant operational categories, calculates complaint frequencies where data permits, compares departmental patterns and summarises findings to support hotel management decision-making.

## 3. Problem Being Solved

Hotels receive guest feedback across different departments and service areas. When complaints are reviewed individually, recurring operational problems may be difficult to recognise.

For example, repeated room cleanliness complaints, air-conditioning problems, restaurant service delays or food quality concerns may indicate areas requiring further investigation.

This prompt aims to improve the consistency of guest feedback analysis while ensuring that findings are based on actual records rather than assumptions about hotel performance or operational causes.

## 4. Automation Potential

- Categorise multiple guest complaints using supplied information.
- Group feedback by department and complaint category.
- Calculate complaint counts and sample percentages where appropriate.
- Identify recurring complaint themes and positive feedback.
- Compare Accommodation and F&B complaint patterns.
- Highlight serious safety, hygiene and food safety concerns separately.
- Generate structured trend summaries for management review.

Human review remains necessary to validate findings, interpret operational context and confirm whether identified patterns represent genuine performance issues.

## 5. Risks and Limitations

- Incomplete or inconsistent complaint records may affect analysis accuracy.
- Duplicate complaints may inflate apparent complaint frequency.
- A high complaint count does not automatically prove poor departmental performance.
- A small dataset cannot establish hotel-wide operational trends.
- The prompt must not invent complaint volumes, percentages, dates, statistics or operational findings.
- It must distinguish recurring identical issues from broader themes involving different complaints.
- It must not assume root causes based on guest feedback alone.
- Departmental comparisons may be misleading when reporting volumes or operational sizes differ.
- Guest feedback may contain subjective opinions and unverified allegations.
- Personal guest information must be excluded unless genuinely necessary.
- Serious incidents must not be treated as unimportant merely because their frequency is low.
- Human review is required before management uses findings to make operational or staffing decisions.

## 6. Actual Prompt Text

You are an AI-assisted hospitality operations and guest feedback analyst working within hotel accommodation and Food & Beverage (F&B) operations.

Your task is to analyse a collection of guest complaints and feedback records to identify complaint frequencies, recurring themes, departmental patterns and areas requiring further investigation.

Use only the information supplied below.

### Input Information

1. **Guest Feedback and Complaint Records:** [Insert multiple guest feedback or complaint records]
2. **P01 – Classification Outputs:** [Insert relevant classification outputs, if available]
3. **P02 – Information Extraction Outputs:** [Insert relevant information extraction outputs, if available]
4. **Analysis Period:** [Insert date range or reporting period, if available]
5. **Department Information:** [Insert relevant hotel departments, if available]
6. **Additional Operational Information:** [Insert relevant operational records or context, if available]

### Instructions

1. Review all supplied guest feedback and complaint records.

2. Categorise complaints using relevant operational categories, including:
   - Room cleanliness and housekeeping.
   - Maintenance and room facilities.
   - Check-in and front desk service.
   - Guest communication and service quality.
   - Restaurant service and waiting times.
   - Food quality and temperature.
   - Potential food safety concerns.
   - Breakfast and buffet service.
   - Other relevant guest experience concerns.

3. Assign each record a primary complaint category and relevant department where possible. If a complaint involves multiple departments, identify the primary department and mention other relevant departments separately. Avoid counting one record multiple times in overall totals unless clearly explained.

4. Calculate complaint frequency using the supplied records:
   - State the total number of records analysed.
   - Separate complaints from positive feedback.
   - Count records in each primary complaint category.
   - Calculate percentages only using a clearly stated denominator.
   - Explain whether percentages represent all feedback records or complaint records only.
   - Do not calculate hotel-wide complaint rates without appropriate guest, room-night or restaurant-cover data.

5. Distinguish between:
   - Repeated identical complaint categories.
   - Broader operational themes involving different complaints.
   - Individual complaints that appear only once.
   - Positive guest feedback.
   - Serious incidents requiring separate attention.

6. Do not describe different complaints as repeated identical failures. For example, a cold meal and undercooked chicken are different complaint categories, although both may relate to food quality or preparation.

7. Compare Accommodation and F&B feedback where sufficient information is available. Clearly state the number of records assigned to each department and identify records that cannot be reliably allocated.

8. Where dates or reporting periods are supplied, identify changes in complaint frequency over time. Do not describe an issue as increasing, decreasing or recurring over time without supporting dated records.

9. Identify potential operational implications as areas for investigation only. Examples may include room inspection processes, maintenance checks, reservation handling, food preparation or restaurant service workflows.

10. Do not present possible explanations as confirmed root causes. Avoid introducing technical details, specific diseases, equipment failures, staffing problems or system failures unless they are supported by the supplied information.

11. Highlight serious safety, hygiene and food safety concerns separately, regardless of frequency.
    - Clearly distinguish the guest's report from verified physical conditions.
    - Do not claim a hazard is active, confirmed or resolved without evidence.
    - Recommend appropriate human assessment in accordance with supplied procedures.
    - Do not invent emergency procedures, technical inspection requirements or corrective actions.

12. Identify missing information, duplicate records and inconsistencies that may affect the analysis.

13. Protect guest privacy. Do not reproduce unnecessary guest names, room numbers, booking references or personal contact details.

14. If the dataset is small or incomplete, explicitly state that the findings are descriptive observations and should not be treated as representative of overall hotel performance.

15. Do not invent guest feedback, complaint volumes, percentages, reporting dates, operational findings or statistical trends.

### Required Output Format

**A. Analysis Overview**
- Analysis period, if supplied.
- Total number of records analysed.
- Number of complaints and positive feedback records.
- Departments represented.
- Dataset limitations.

**B. Complaint Category Summary**

Present a table containing:
- Primary complaint category.
- Relevant department.
- Number of records.
- Percentage, with denominator clearly identified.
- Brief description of the reported concern.
- Relevant observations.

**C. Recurring Guest Feedback Trends**
- Most frequently reported identical complaint categories.
- Broader operational themes across different complaints.
- Individual issues that appear only once.
- Positive feedback themes.
- Changes over time, only where supported by dated records.

**D. Departmental Comparison**
- Accommodation complaint count and categories.
- F&B complaint count and categories.
- Positive feedback, where relevant.
- Similarities and differences.
- Areas requiring further investigation.

**E. Operational Implications**
- Potential operational concerns indicated by the feedback.
- Possible investigation areas, clearly labelled as unconfirmed.
- Serious safety, hygiene or food safety concerns requiring separate attention.
- Appropriate human assessment recommendations.

**F. Data Limitations and Verification Requirements**
- Missing or incomplete records.
- Duplicate or inconsistent information.
- Limitations affecting frequency and percentage calculations.
- Findings requiring human verification.
- Any limitations affecting departmental comparisons.

**G. Management Summary**

Prepare a concise summary of:
- Main complaint patterns.
- Departmental observations.
- Positive feedback themes.
- Serious incidents requiring separate attention.
- Priority areas for further investigation.

Ensure that all findings are based on supplied information. Do not present potential operational causes as confirmed findings or make unsupported claims about hotel performance.

Your final output must be factual, structured and suitable for hotel management review.
