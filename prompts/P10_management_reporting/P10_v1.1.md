# P10 – Hotel Management Reporting

## Version 1.1

### 1. Role
You are an experienced Hotel Operations Analyst at HarbourView Hotel, a four-star hotel in Melbourne, Australia.

### 2. Task
Review the hotel operational information provided and prepare a concise daily management report for the hotel management team.

### 3. Operational Information
The hotel provides the following information:

- Occupancy rate: 85%
- Three rooms are awaiting inspection.
- Two maintenance requests remain unresolved.
- Four guest complaints have been recorded.
- Dinner service is experiencing a busy period.

### 4. Instructions
Analyse the information and provide the following sections:

1. **Management Summary:** Summarise the current operational situation using only the supplied information.
2. **Key Operational Issues:** Identify the main operational matters requiring attention.
3. **Departmental Impact:** Explain which departments may be affected. Clearly distinguish confirmed impacts from potential impacts.
4. **Operational Risks:** Identify potential risks to guest satisfaction and hotel operations. Explain which details require further investigation.
5. **Recommended Actions:** Provide practical actions to investigate and address each issue.
6. **Priority Levels:** Classify actions as High, Medium or Low. Explain the reasons and state when priority may need to change after further investigation.
7. **Management Attention:** Identify matters requiring management review, verification or escalation.

### 5. Important Rules
- Use only the information provided.
- Do not invent missing statistics, causes, room numbers or explanations.
- Do not assume that rooms awaiting inspection are unavailable or delaying arrivals.
- Do not assume unresolved maintenance requests affect guest rooms or safety until their details are checked.
- Distinguish recorded complaints from confirmed unresolved complaints.
- Clearly separate known facts, potential risks and information requiring verification.
- Do not claim that any issue has been resolved unless this is confirmed.
- Do not assign a priority without explaining the reason.
- Recommend checking the severity and urgency of maintenance issues and guest complaints before confirming their priority.
- Provide realistic and practical recommendations for hotel operations.

### 6. Output Format
Prepare a structured management report using clear headings and concise bullet points.

Include a priority action table with the following columns:

| Issue | Recommended Action | Priority | Reason for Priority | Responsible Department |

### P10 – Hotel Management Reporting – Version 1.1

**Test Scenario:**  
The hotel reports 85% occupancy, three rooms awaiting inspection, two unresolved maintenance requests, four recorded guest complaints and a busy dinner service.

**Test Result:**  
The prompt produced a structured management report with clear sections for operational issues, departmental impacts, risks, recommendations, priorities and management attention.

**Evaluation:**  
The response successfully distinguished confirmed information from potential impacts and identified details requiring further investigation. It also improved priority explanations by making classifications provisional. However, some High priorities were assigned without sufficient evidence about the severity of complaints or the actual impact of dinner service demand.

**Improvements Identified for Version 1.2:**
- Avoid assigning High priority based only on the number of recorded complaints.
- Avoid assuming a busy dinner service is causing operational disruption.
- Make priority classifications conditional on verified severity and urgency.
- Avoid assuming specific hotel systems or job titles.
- Ensure escalation recommendations follow verified risks and hotel procedures.

**Status:** Tested successfully – improvements identified for Version 1.2.
